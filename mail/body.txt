BUSINESS IDEAS
1. Lab and prescription digitiser for Indian clinics with a clinical glossary (score 4.2; effort 3, cost 2, fit 5)
   A per-clinic SaaS that reads printed and scanned lab reports and prescriptions, maps terms through a glossary of Indian clinical vocabulary and abbreviations, and sends structured fields to the clinic's existing system for the doctor to confirm. Priced monthly per doctor seat, with no clinical decisions made by the tool.
   Why now: Generated domain glossaries are now a cheap way to feed specialised vocabulary into extraction, and Fowler's write-up on that method fits clinic report text that generic OCR models miss.
   First step: Ask a clinic you already support for 30 de-identified lab report scans, then label the fields front-desk staff re-type most often to set your first yield baseline.

2. Employee medical reimbursement payouts for Indian SMEs (score 4.0; effort 3, cost 3, fit 5)
   A B2B tool where HR uploads employee medical and benefit claims, receipts are checked automatically, and approved amounts are paid out by UPI or IMPS through a licensed payout partner. It includes an audit trail and bank account validation, and is sold per employee per month to companies with 50 to 500 staff.
   Why now: Flex's engineer #1 story shows payment friction in HSA and FSA-style benefits is still unsolved, and Indian SMEs have no equivalent of those US rails.
   First step: Map the claim-to-payout flow at two mid-size Indian employers you know, then price ten test payouts through a licensed partner's sandbox API.

3. Behaviour-as-data test harness for AI-written backend code (score 4.0; effort 3, cost 1, fit 4)
   A CI add-on where customer-level behaviour tests are stored as YAML scenarios outside the codebase, run against every agent-written pull request, and report which behaviours changed. It targets Java and Spring Boot shops that let coding agents write much of their code, and is sold as a flat monthly plan.
   Why now: Fowler's argument for storing tests as data so agents cannot edit them, together with Shopify's account of agent-written app rebuilds, shows teams need a contract that agent edits cannot change.
   First step: Convert ten endpoint tests from an open-source Spring Boot repo into a YAML scenario file, and run them in GitHub Actions against a pull request an agent opened.

4. Durable follow-up workflows for clinics and diagnostic labs (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   A small engine that runs multi-day follow-up flows for clinics, such as a pending lab report, a missed review, or a recall visit. Each step is logged so a crash or restart resumes where it stopped, and staff see exactly which messages were sent. The doctor writes and approves all templates. Sold per clinic per month.
   Why now: Fowler's explanation of durable execution for long-running agents gives a clear pattern for follow-ups that span days and external systems, and clinics currently rely on manual calls.
   First step: Write a one-page spec for three follow-up flows, then build a Spring Boot demo that forces a crash mid-flow and resumes from the step log.

5. Scoped agent for hospital billing desks that work through TPA and payer portals (score 3.5; effort 4, cost 2, fit 4)
   A governed agent layer that lets a hospital billing team run pre-authorisation and claim-status checks on insurer or TPA portals that have no API. Every action runs under per-portal permissions, is logged for audit, and can be stopped instantly. Sold as a monthly subscription to mid-size hospitals.
   Why now: Browser-based agents are now being tested against payer portals and EHRs by large players, and the way Pomerado turns sites without APIs into agent tools shows buyers want this, but without permissions and audit it stays risky in healthcare.
   First step: Read the terms of one TPA portal a hospital you know uses, and ask its billing lead to list the three pre-auth steps staff repeat most every day.
