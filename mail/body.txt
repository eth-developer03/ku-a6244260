BUSINESS IDEAS
1. Per-step LLM cost auditor for agent pipelines (score 4.2; effort 2, cost 1, fit 4)
   A tool that records token use and latency for every step in an agent pipeline and suggests where a cheaper model, a small specialised decider or caching would do the job. It is sold as a monthly report to startups that spend more than a set amount on LLM APIs.
   Why now: Amazon reportedly saved millions by replacing Sonnet 4.6 with a specialised decider, and Walmart cut inference costs from over $100k to $10k a month by moving to pay-as-you-go, so cost-per-step data now has a clear payoff.
   First step: Add per-step token and latency logging to one of your existing agent pipelines and total the cost of each step for a week.

2. Employee reimbursement payout API for Indian SMEs over UPI and IMPS (score 4.0; effort 3, cost 3, fit 5)
   An API that lets Indian SMEs pay medical, travel and benefit reimbursements straight to employee bank accounts, with account validation before each payout and a reconciliation report for finance. It is sold per payout or as a flat monthly plan, and it runs through a licensed payment partner rather than as a standalone payment license.
   Why now: Canada's Real-Time Rail launch shows national real-time rails becoming the default, and Flex's engineer-number-one story on fixing HSA and FSA payments shows benefit payouts are still painful.
   First step: Talk to three finance or HR leads at Indian SMEs about how they pay medical reimbursements today, and compare two bank-account validation and payout partners' pricing and onboarding.

3. Durable step log library for long-running agent workflows (score 4.0; effort 3, cost 1, fit 4)
   A small open-source library, in Java or Python, that journals each external tool call and its result to Postgres or SQLite. After a crash, it skips completed steps and rebuilds state from the log. The paid version is a hosted runner for small teams.
   Why now: Martin Fowler's piece on durable execution for multi-day agents and Rivet's S3-backed hibernating actors both point to the same unmet need: agents that survive crashes without a heavy orchestrator.
   First step: Build a roughly 100-line Python decorator that journals tool results to SQLite and replays them after a simulated crash, then post it as a Show HN.

4. Closed-loop diagnostic report tracker for clinics and diagnostic labs (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   A SaaS that tracks every pending diagnostic report, sends reminders to patients over WhatsApp or SMS, and logs when the referring doctor has acknowledged the result. It gives clinics an audit trail that shows the follow-up loop was closed.
   Why now: An NCDRC ruling on medical negligence put the burden of collecting diagnostic reports and coordinating follow-up on hospitals and doctors, so clinics now need evidence that the loop was closed.
   First step: Map the full lifecycle of one diagnostic report at a clinic you know, from order to patient collection to doctor acknowledgement, and mark every handoff where a report can be lost.

5. Discharge summary drafter for small private hospitals in India (score 3.5; effort 4, cost 2, fit 4)
   A tool that drafts discharge summaries from admission notes, lab results and medication orders, with the treating doctor reviewing and signing every draft. Sold per bed-month to hospitals with 30 to 150 beds that lack a documentation team.
   Why now: A new npj Digital Medicine study found AI can help produce discharge summaries for long, complex stays, which makes a doctor-reviewed drafting workflow credible to hospital administrators now.
   First step: Ask two hospital contacts from your internship for ten anonymised discharge summaries and time how long each one takes a doctor to write.
