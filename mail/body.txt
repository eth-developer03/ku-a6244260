BUSINESS IDEAS
1. Java library blast-radius checker as a GitHub App (score 4.5; effort 3, cost 1, fit 5)
   A GitHub App that, on each Maven or Gradle dependency bump or CVE patch PR, scans the other repos in the same org and comments with the consumer call sites that could break. It is sold per organisation and mainly targets Indian product companies running many Spring Boot services.
   Why now: With Java4Shell now public and Cloudflare shipping WAF rules, teams need to patch fast without knowing which services will break, and Netflix's cross-repo impact tool shows the approach works.
   First step: Write a script that uses the JavaParser library to find which methods in a sample Spring Boot library are called across five of your own repos, and check how close the output is to a manual audit.

2. On-prem clinical note drafting appliance for small clinics and nursing homes (score 3.8; effort 4, cost 3, fit 5)
   A Docker-packaged, offline-capable tool that drafts visit notes and discharge summaries from the doctor's dictation or structured inputs, runs on a single clinic server, and never sends patient data to a third-party model API. The doctor reviews and signs every draft. Sold as a one-time licence plus support to small clinics.
   Why now: Reports of data processing incidents at inference providers make clinics wary of sending patient data to hosted model APIs, which creates demand for local deployment.
   First step: Benchmark a quantised open-weight speech-to-text and small LLM on a single 16 GB GPU or Mac mini with 20 synthetic clinic dictations, and record latency and draft quality.

3. Clinical AI pre-deployment evaluation kit for Indian hospitals (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   A self-hosted harness that lets a hospital run a vendor's clinical AI tool against its own de-identified past cases and produce a reproducible scorecard, with sensitivity, specificity and subgroup breakdowns by site. Hospitals pay per evaluation or an annual re-run subscription, and the clinical team decides whether to adopt.
   Why now: Health systems are being warned that vendor exam scores don't reflect real-world performance, and a multi-institutional grading study shows how much validation depends on the local setting.
   First step: Interview two hospital IT or quality leads you know from your internship and ask how they currently check vendor AI claims before buying.

4. Employee medical and meal reimbursement payouts for Indian SMEs (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   An API and dashboard where a company's HR or finance team uploads or approves claims, validates the employee's bank account, and pays out via IMPS or UPI through a licensed payout partner. It targets 20 to 500-person companies that currently handle these claims through manual payroll runs. You earn a per-payout fee and avoid holding funds yourself.
   Why now: Flex's engineer #1 story shows that HSA and FSA payment friction is painful enough to justify a company, and that gap persists in India's SME benefits market.
   First step: Map the fees and turnaround times of two licensed payout APIs in India and draft a per-payout pricing sheet for a 100-employee company.

5. Warm sandbox pool broker for coding-agent sessions (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   A lightweight broker that keeps a pool of warm, per-team VMs in the customer's own cloud account and assigns them to agent sessions, so environments are not reserved for each full conversation. It is priced per session-hour for small Indian dev agencies running coding agents. The focus is on cutting the environment cost of idle agent time.
   Why now: Research shows agent sessions hold full environments while waiting on model replies, and an open-source Devin alternative reports that per-team VMs beat per-session sandboxes past about 640 session-hours a month.
   First step: Measure your own agent runs by logging how long each session holds its environment versus how long it actively calls the model, then compute the idle share.
