BUSINESS IDEAS
1. Yield dashboard for clinical document digitisation (score 4.8; effort 2, cost 1, fit 5)
   A small service that wraps any OCR or LLM extraction pipeline for scanned prescriptions and lab reports and reports yield, meaning the share of records that pass deterministic checks and reach the EHR, next to accuracy. Sold per document batch to digitisation vendors and hospital IT teams.
   Why now: A new benchmark shows voting and self-correction mainly withhold doubtful records rather than fix them, so teams need to see yield to know what their pipeline is actually delivering.
   First step: Take 200 anonymised scanned lab reports from an existing internship project and measure yield with deterministic lookup checks against the lab's test-code list.

2. Durable-execution starter for Spring Boot agent jobs (score 4.5; effort 3, cost 1, fit 5)
   A small Spring Boot library that records each external tool call result in Postgres, so a crashed multi-day agent job resumes from its last completed step without repeating side effects. Sold as an open-source core with a paid hosted dashboard for monitoring jobs.
   Why now: Martin Fowler's piece on durable execution for long-running agents and Rivet's S3-backed actor orchestrator both show teams building this same primitive from scratch.
   First step: Build a prototype that wraps three tool calls in a Spring Boot service and checks that a forced crash between steps resumes without duplicate writes.

3. Employee benefits claim-to-payout API for Indian HR teams (score 4.2; effort 3, cost 2, fit 5)
   A solo-built API and HR dashboard that handles medical and meal-benefit claim submission, receipt checks, approval rules and payouts to employee bank accounts through a licensed payout partner. You charge per claim or a monthly seat fee and never hold funds yourself.
   Why now: Flex's founder built the company after running into HSA and FSA payment problems, which shows that employers and employees feel benefit payout friction directly.
   First step: Email five HR managers at 50 to 300-person Indian companies and ask how they currently reimburse medical claims, then record the steps and delays they describe.

4. Pending-report tracker for Indian clinics and labs (score 4.2; effort 2, cost 1, fit 4)
   A WhatsApp and SMS workflow tool that tracks every ordered test from order to report collection, sends reminders, and keeps a timestamped log of each outreach attempt. Clinics and diagnostic labs pay a monthly per-location fee, and the doctor still reviews every report.
   Why now: A new NCDRC ruling makes hospitals and doctors answerable for whether diagnostic reports actually reached the patient, so a documented closed loop now has legal value.
   First step: Interview three clinic front-desk staff this week about how they currently chase pending reports, and write down the exact steps they take.

5. Pre-authorisation packet checker for Indian hospital billing desks (score 4.0; effort 3, cost 1, fit 4)
   A rules engine that checks insurance pre-auth and claim packets against the TPA's documented requirements before submission, using an LLM only to extract fields from scanned documents. Hospitals pay per claim checked, and the rules stay deterministic and auditable.
   Why now: Workflow-distillation work shows that SOPs and logged runs can become deterministic step sequences, which makes checks on repeated billing steps cheaper and easier to audit than an LLM agent.
   First step: Collect the pre-auth checklists from two TPAs' public provider manuals and turn them into a rule file with one pass/fail rule per required field.
