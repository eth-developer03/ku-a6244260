BUSINESS IDEAS
1. Customer-level acceptance tests stored as data for AI-written Spring Boot code (score 4.8; effort 2, cost 1, fit 5)
   A CLI and GitHub Action where a team keeps its customer-level acceptance scenarios as YAML fixtures outside the code, so an AI agent cannot edit them. The tool runs them on every agent-authored pull request and posts a pass/fail summary with the failing scenario. Sold as a low-cost team plan to Indian product startups.
   Why now: Fowler's piece on tests as data argues that customer-level tests must sit outside what the agent can edit, and a study of coding-agent patches finds they run larger than developers' and add extra test edits.
   First step: Write ten customer-level scenarios as YAML for one of your own services and run them against a real agent-generated pull request.

2. Pending-follow-up tracker with WhatsApp reminders for Indian clinics (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   A small SaaS where clinic staff log doctor-ordered follow-ups (repeat tests, review visits, pending reports) and the system sends scheduled WhatsApp reminders to patients and shows open loops on a dashboard for the doctor. It contains no clinical logic; the doctor decides what gets ordered. Sold per clinic per month.
   Why now: Beacon Health's push to stop patient care slipping through gaps shows that closing follow-up loops is a live problem, and Indian clinics mostly track it on paper or in memory.
   First step: Interview five front-desk staff at clinics in your internship network about how they track pending follow-ups today, and write down the exact steps.

3. Audit trail and sign-off tracker for AI-drafted radiology reports (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   A layer for diagnostic centres that sits between an AI draft and radiologist sign-off. It stores each draft, every edit, and the sign-off timestamp and user, and checks that required report sections are present before release. The radiologist remains the signer. Sold as a per-centre subscription.
   Why now: STAT's coverage of RSNA debates AI-drafted radiology reports ahead of the conference, so centres will need a provable record of who wrote and approved each report.
   First step: Ask one radiologist you know to walk you through their current report sign-off flow, and note where edits and approvals get lost.

4. Approval-gated employee reimbursement payouts for Indian SMBs (score 3.5; effort 3, cost 3, fit 4)
   A thin app where an employee submits a claim, a manager approves it, and the payout goes out by UPI or IMPS through a licensed Indian payout provider. The product handles the workflow, approval audit trail, and reconciliation, not the banking licence. It targets SMBs that currently pay claims through manual bank transfers.
   Why now: Versine's MCP launch lets agents send money from a wallet with passkey-based user approval and cash-out to bank in about 2 minutes, which sets the expectation for fast, approved payouts.
   First step: Sign up for a sandbox with a licensed Indian payout API and map one claim-to-payout flow end to end in a script.

5. Cost-matrix fraud scoring for small Indian lenders and marketplaces (score 3.2; effort 4, cost 3, fit 4)
   A hosted or self-hostable service that scores transactions and picks decision thresholds by minimising expected cost, using amount-weighted losses rather than fixed cutoffs. It gives small lenders and marketplaces an open alternative to expensive enterprise fraud engines. Priced per transaction volume.
   Why now: A new arXiv paper shows decision-focused fine-tuning beating post-hoc Bayes thresholds on amount-scaled fraud costs, and a report of WebRTC card skimmers on shopping sites makes payment fraud feel current.
   First step: Reproduce the paper's threshold-versus-decision-focused comparison on a public fraud dataset and record the cost difference.
