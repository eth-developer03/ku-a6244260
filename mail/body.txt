BUSINESS IDEAS
1. Per-feature LLM cost attribution for AI product teams (score 4.8; effort 2, cost 1, fit 5)
   A small SDK and dashboard that tags token spend by customer and feature, then suggests cheaper model routes for each task based on measured quality. Sold to Indian SaaS teams whose LLM bills are growing faster than revenue.
   Why now: Shopify describes token-heavy workloads driving it to post-train smaller task-specific models, and new fast model variants are landing, so teams now need per-task cost data to choose between them.
   First step: Tag every LLM call in one of your own pipelines with feature and tenant IDs, then compare cost per completed task across two models for a week.

2. DischargeDraft: discharge summary drafting for small Indian hospitals (score 4.2; effort 3, cost 2, fit 5)
   A tool that drafts discharge summaries from exported EMR notes and lab PDFs, which the treating doctor reviews and signs. Sold per bed per month to 20 to 100 bed private hospitals that currently spend staff hours on summaries.
   Why now: A new study shows AI can improve discharge summaries for long, complex stays, so hospitals now have evidence that drafting help is safe to try.
   First step: Interview five staff members from your internship sites about their current discharge summary template, turnaround time, and what they copy-paste most.

3. Scoped, revocable permissions and audit logs for AI agents (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   A hosted proxy that gives each AI agent narrow, revocable access to third-party APIs, with a kill switch and exportable audit logs. Sold monthly to small Indian startups running agents against payment, CRM, or accounting APIs.
   Why now: An open-source OAuth broker for agents just launched, and Fowler notes that the gap between building and running agents in production is what makes teams hesitate.
   First step: Set up the open-source agent identity broker locally, then write down the three operational gaps you hit, such as audit export, per-team policies, or alerting.

4. Regression QA agent for Indian checkout and payment flows (score 3.8; effort 3, cost 2, fit 4)
   A GitHub Action that runs an AI agent through a SaaS app's signup, checkout, and payment pages, including UPI intent and net banking redirects, and reports regressions on each pull request. Sold per repo per month to small Indian SaaS and D2C teams without QA staff.
   Why now: Momentic's agents now explore apps to find bugs, and Meta reports LLM-generated tests catching bugs before code lands, so the approach is being validated at both startup and big-company scale.
   First step: Pick one open-source Indian e-commerce demo app, write an agent script for its checkout, and measure how many breaking changes it catches across ten commits.

5. On-prem report digitiser for diagnostic labs and nursing homes (score 3.0; effort 4, cost 4, fit 4)
   A single-box appliance that runs OCR and a small open-weight model locally to turn lab and scan reports into structured records, with no data leaving the premises. Sold as a one-time hardware plus annual support package to diagnostic labs and nursing homes worried about data protection.
   Why now: YC is actively calling for on-prem frontier AI for air-gapped sectors like hospitals, which signals buyers will pay for local deployment.
   First step: Benchmark one open OCR model plus one small open LLM on a laptop against 20 anonymised reports you are permitted to use, and record accuracy and latency.
